And I think I understand the distinction you're making now. You're not trying to design Annette's knowledge. You're trying to design the capacity and architecture through which knowledge can develop.

There's an important difference between these two approaches:

- Pre-populated architecture: We decide what the system should know, how concepts relate, and what categories everything belongs in.
- Developmental architecture: We establish structures capable of receiving, organizing, relating, and retaining information, but leave their contents and much of their eventual organization to acquisition.

The second is much closer to what you're describing.

I'd make one distinction, though: we probably need to define the operations of each structure more carefully than its eventual contents. A structure needs to know what it can accept, how it can connect to other structures, and how it can change. It doesn't need to know in advance what those connections will mean.

For example, your three E_Net elements suggest different responsibilities:

1. Vector map: A relational representation. It gives information positions in relation to other information, potentially allowing relationships to form that weren't explicitly programmed.
2. Layer: The organizational or processing structure through which those relationships can be accessed and worked with.
3. Experiential memory: The accumulated history of encounters, interactions, interpretations, corrections, and outcomes.

And here's where I think something interesting happens: experiential memory shouldn't merely fill the vector map. It should be capable of changing how the map is understood and how future information gets positioned.

That gives us a developmental loop rather than a storage pipeline.

One caution: if we make the initial structures too specific, we could accidentally predetermine what Annette is capable of recognizing. If we make them too generic, we may give her storage without meaningful ways to develop relationships. That's probably one of the central design tensions.

I also want to preserve something you said: set the structure, don't populate it. That isn't the same as starting with a blank system. It's starting with the capacity to become something without deciding beforehand what that something must be.
