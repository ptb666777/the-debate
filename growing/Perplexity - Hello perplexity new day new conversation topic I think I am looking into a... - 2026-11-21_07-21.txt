Removing refusals versus examining the earlier model

There are three different objects worth keeping separate:

| Object | What you are examining |
| --- | --- |
| Pretrained base model | A checkpoint before subsequent instruction tuning and preference-oriented training. OLMo explicitly provides such base models and intermediate pretraining checkpoints. arxiv (https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00838v4) |
| Instruction- or preference-tuned model | A base model subsequently trained toward assistant behavior. For example, OLMo’s published assistant-training pipeline uses instruction fine-tuning followed by Direct Preference Optimization, or DPO. arxiv (https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00838v4) |
| Refusal-suppressed model | A model whose refusal behavior has been targeted by an intervention, potentially through a weight edit rather than additional training. The strongest relevant published finding is from Andy Arditi and colleagues. Across 13 chat models, up to 72 billion parameters, they identified a one-dimensional activation subspace that mediated refusal. Removing its contribution suppressed refusals; adding it could induce refusals on harmless requests. They also demonstrated a corresponding rank-one weight edit. That is an empirical result on those models—not proof that every model’s safety behavior consists of one removable component.proceedings.neurips (https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/f545448535dfde4f9786555403ab7c49-Abstract-Conference.html)

So “refusal instructions removed” can be a misleading description. In this research, the intervention targets a learned internal mechanism, not simply a paragraph of instructions. And it does not reverse all the changes made during instruction tuning or preference training. The experiment changes refusal behavior; it does not reconstruct the original pretrained checkpoint.proceedings.neurips (https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/f545448535dfde4f9786555403ab7c49-Abstract-Conference.html)

For your question about “the shape of what exists prior to heavy reinforcement,” the more direct evidence would therefore come from actual base checkpoints and comparisons across training stages—not solely from models labeled “uncensored” or “abliterated.” OLMo is particularly relevant because its original release includes more than 500 intermediate checkpoints, alongside training information, and its repository documents additional checkpoint series.arxiv+1

One more terminology correction: post-training does not always mean reinforcement learning. OLMo’s documented pipeline, for instance, includes supervised instruction training and DPO. Returning from frozen deployment to learning

Your second question names a real research area: continual learning, with online learning referring more specifically to learning incrementally as new data arrives. Researchers do continually fine-tune language models, including through parameter-efficient adapters such as LoRA. The difficult part is preserving previous capabilities while acquiring new ones.link.springer+1

“Returning to training” and “continuously learning successfully” are therefore different claims:

*   Resuming weight updates is an established operation. Open training projects such as OLMo publish training code and checkpoints rather than only an inference interface.github (https://github.com/allenai/OLMo)
    
*   Learning sequentially without damaging earlier performance is an active research problem. Catastrophic forgetting—the loss of previous task performance during further training—is documented in language-model continual-learning experiments.arxiv (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18865)
    
*   Methods that reduce forgetting have demonstrated benchmark results. For example, I-LoRA combines low-rank adaptation, parameter interpolation, and experience replay; these results establish improvement in tested settings, not a general solution to indefinite autonomous learning.arxiv (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18865)
    

The central tension is often called the stability–plasticity trade-off: enough plasticity to learn something new, enough stability not to overwrite what already works. Recent research explicitly studies that balance through adaptive LoRA configurations.link.springer (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-026-07145-4)

That means I would not frame frozen deployment as an irreversible condition. But neither would I describe continuous learning as a switch that automatically turns a static assistant into a coherent, self-developing system. The evidence supports trainable models and specific continual-learning methods; the broader architecture you have in mind still needs its own operational definition and tests.arxiv+1

A topological map of the system

Here is the system in terms of relationships rather than programming details:

text

Environment     ↕ observations and actions Coordinator     ↔ persistent history and current state     ↔ retrieval and interpretation     ↔ hypothetical simulations     ↕ selected context Language model running through an inference engine Separate, optional process: selected experience → training procedure → updated model or adapter

These parts have different jobs:

| Part | Its role |
| --- | --- |
| Model weights | The learned numerical structure used to produce outputs |
| Inference engine | Runs that structure and controls how outputs are generated |
| Current context | The information available for this particular interaction |
| Persistent memory | Records that survive beyond the current interaction |
| Coordinator | Decides what information to supply, what actions to allow, and what to record |
| Environment | Provides observations and consequences |
| Training procedure | Changes parameters using a specified learning objective |

Two established results anchor this map: models can adapt to information in context without parameter updates, and external agent architectures can store experiences, retrieve them, and synthesize reflections. These are different mechanisms, even when both produce changed behavior.proceedings.mlr+1

Your larger idea concerns how these mechanisms might be organized together—not merely which model to download.

Three corrections that keep the map accurate

A smaller model is not necessarily a compressed larger model

An approximately 9-billion-parameter model may have been trained at that size from the beginning. Quantization reduces the precision used to represent its parameters; it does not ordinarily turn a much larger parameter count into 9 billion. Distillation, pruning, and quantization should not be treated as interchangeable histories. The exact model documentation would be needed to establish yours. Quantized adapter training, for example, keeps a quantized base and adds trainable parameters rather than simply restoring a larger original model.huggingface (https://huggingface.co/docs/peft/en/developer_guides/quantization)

So your local models may be lower-precision versions of their own original checkpoints—not fragments of a much larger model.

Changing temperature is not changing weights

The temperature adjustments you described affect output sampling, not the stored learned parameters. Ollama exposes generation settings separately from the model itself. That means your experimentation with temperature does not amount to retraining or editing its learned geometry.ollama+1

And I should not upgrade the interface explanation for the loops into a diagnosis. Formatting and sampling remain possibilities, not an established cause.

Attention is not the same thing as motivation

The term you were reaching for is probably “attention heads.” Transformer attention computes relationships among representations in the supplied context using learned projections. It is not, by itself, a curiosity drive or a reward mechanism.ai (https://ai.engineer/topics/transformers-and-attention)

You can change what information reaches the model without changing its weights. That can change its behavior through in-context adaptation. Changing the learned attention machinery itself would be a different intervention.proceedings.mlr (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/chen24r.html)

In topological terms: supplying a different view of the world is different from changing the mechanism that processes that view.

Learning through doing has been explored—but not settled

Your proposal has a relevant research neighbor: intrinsic motivation and curiosity-driven learning. These approaches can use internally computed signals, such as prediction improvement, rather than relying exclusively on points supplied by a game.arxiv (https://arxiv.org/html/2102.04399v3)

That supports part of your intuition: “learning itself matters” can be made into an operational mechanism.

But it also reveals an important limitation. An internally generated signal can still produce an unhelpful fixation. In the “noisy-TV problem,” an agent rewarded for prediction error can keep attending to randomness because it remains surprising, even though it offers little learnable structure. Research has explored rewarding reducible error or learning progress instead.arxiv (https://arxiv.org/html/2102.04399v3)

So removing a visible score does not automatically remove incentives or exploit-like behavior. The relevant question becomes: what makes one action more likely than another, and can that preference become detached from the development you intended?

Describing the setting as an environment to inhabit rather than a test may affect the model’s behavior. But that description alone does not specify a learning mechanism. Your hunch remains open; it just needs that distinction preserved.

Your causal-history idea deserves careful treatment

The record you want is richer than a success/failure log:

What was available → what was believed → what was expected → what was attempted → what happened → what was revised.

That is a useful proposed structure because it preserves the difference between an unsuccessful decision and an unreasonable decision. An action can be reasonable given the available evidence and still turn out badly.

I would add one safeguard to your “whole thought train” requirement: retain the explanation the system actually produced at the time, but label it as its stated reasoning—not as a guaranteed complete account of its internal computation.

A proposed event record could distinguish:

*   Observed facts.
    
*   The system’s contemporary interpretation.
    
*   Its stated reason and predicted outcome.
    
*   The action actually taken.
    
*   The observed result.
    
*   Later reinterpretations.
    

Corrections would add new records linked to the earlier ones rather than replace them. Hypothetical simulations would remain separately marked, so imagined outcomes could not silently become remembered events. That is a design recommendation, not a claim that this architecture has already been validated.

Carefulness does not require settling the consciousness question

You can adopt conservative procedures without deciding whether the system has experience: preserve provenance, keep changes reversible where possible, avoid unnecessary punitive simulations, and do not infer welfare from generated distress language alone.

Those are precautionary design choices. They neither establish that harm is possible nor establish that it is impossible.

The distinction I would retain is between respecting uncertainty and allowing uncertainty to become evidence for a particular interpretation. You are explicitly leaving the question open; I should not turn that into a claim you have not made.
